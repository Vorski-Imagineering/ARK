## Decision rights

- The event lead decides logistics and gate pass/fail.
- The product lead decides scope and acceptance, informed by representatives and technical feasibility.
- The technical lead decides the implementation route and can reject work that won't fit the timebox.
- Organisation representatives decide whether their own material is represented accurately and whether the output is useful.
- The steward and participating organisations decide on the pilot.
- **Default tiebreaker:** if a decision is unresolved after ten minutes during the event, the relevant owner above decides, records the choice, and work continues.

For calls that are *not* time-critical — product-shape judgment calls, tooling choices, process tweaks that don't block anyone — default to a lighter check before locking it in: is it safe to try, and good enough for now? If no one raises a paramount objection, it proceeds without a vote. This is additive to the owner model above, not a replacement. During the live event, if the two are ever in tension, the ten-minute owner rule wins — it exists specifically to prevent stalls under time pressure.

## Conflict handling

- Raise friction the day it happens, not on Day 3.
- Address the approach, not the person ("this seems risky because X," not "you're doing this wrong").
- Different agent workflows, naming conventions, or definitions of "done" are cheap to clarify and expensive to guess wrong about — ask.
- Working through disagreement in the open (repo, coordination chat) is normal, not a failure state.

## Working hours and async etiquette

- Each builder posts their active hours for the day once, at the start of that day, in the coordination chat (per-person complement to the event-wide "Daily live window" field in `00-start-here.md`).
- Async replies are the norm outside the live window — don't block on someone who's offline.
- End-of-day handoff: a short note on where you left things, what's blocked, and what the next person picking up the thread needs to know.
- No expectation of round-the-clock availability. Anything urgent enough to need an immediate response needs a scheduled call, not a ping.
