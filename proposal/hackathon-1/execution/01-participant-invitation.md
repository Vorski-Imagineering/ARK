## Short invitation

## Full invitation

**Subject:** Join the ARC Agent remote build

Hello,

We previously discussed the idea of an ARC Agent: a shared AI companion that helps people understand what participating organisations are doing and where their work connects.

We are now proposing a concrete next step—a two-week remote ramp-up followed by a focused two-day hackathon. A small working agent will be running before the ramp-up begins. We will use it during preparation and improve that same agent together rather than waiting for the build days to begin.

### What we will attempt

The hackathon will focus on one complete experience:

Public information from at least three organisations  
→ one shared knowledge surface  
→ sourced questions and answers across the organisations  
→ one cross-organisation digest  
→ delivery through one accessible channel  
→ accuracy and usefulness review by participating representatives.

We are not trying to build the complete platform in two days. Automation, personalisation, deep Telegram participation, member profiles, many integrations, and production infrastructure will wait unless they are already available and directly support the central proof.

### How the agent helps organise the work

During the two-week preparation period, the working agent will help participants:

- Find and understand the public proposal, schedule, scope, and decisions.
- Check source packs for missing information and inaccessible public links.
- Draft source-linked test questions and the working hackathon brief.
- Summarise approved public decisions, actions, and known limitations.
- Test the evolving source-to-answer-to-digest flow before the build days.

Human organisers and organisation representatives remain responsible for commitments, permissions, accuracy, publication approval, scope, and readiness decisions. The agent will not be given private contact details, credentials, private meeting transcripts, or non-public organisational information.

### Why your participation matters

This cannot be designed credibly by a technical team using imaginary organisations. Participating organisations provide the public material, decide what would be useful, check whether the system represents their work accurately, and judge whether the result deserves a short pilot.

### What we ask from each organisation

**Required**

- Nominate one organisation representative who can explain and verify its public information.
- Join the 60-minute orientation and 90-minute design-lock sessions.
- Provide a short public organisation profile.
- Provide one to three stable public sources that can be accessed, summarised, and cited for the experiment.
- Identify one practical question or outcome that would make the experiment worthwhile.
- Provide representative coverage for the two build days—throughout where possible, or at the agreed opening, testing, and final demonstration checkpoints.
- Review the prototype for accuracy and usefulness.
- Use the working agent during preparation and report material errors or gaps.
- Join the continuation decision and a short post-event review.

**Optional**

- Nominate a technical, design, facilitation, documentation, or community contributor.
- Offer hosting, communication support, or modest shared costs according to capacity.
- Suggest additional public sources after the core source pack is working.

An organisation does not need to provide a technical contributor. Its representative’s source knowledge, product judgment, testing, and corrections are necessary contributions.

### Approximate commitment

For an organisation representative:

- 30–45 minutes to complete and check the source pack.
- 150 minutes across the two required pre-events.
- Agreed coverage during the two build days; full attendance is preferred, but defined checkpoints are acceptable when necessary.
- 45 minutes for the post-event review.

For a builder:

- The two required pre-events.
- The 60-minute technical readiness session.
- Both build days.
- Any explicitly accepted setup task before Day 1.

### What participants receive

- Direct influence over the first working version.
- Early access to a working agent during the two-week preparation period.
- A shared prototype built from real organisational material.
- Better visibility into participating organisations and possible connections.
- Reusable public documentation and code where applicable.
- Evidence about usefulness, effort, limitations, and whether a pilot is worthwhile.
