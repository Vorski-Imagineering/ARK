§ 01
Mandate What & Why

A working group on memory.

ARK is a small study group focused on agentic recall and knowledge — the practical art of giving AI agents and the humans who work with them durable memory, defensible reasoning, and a shared base they can extend without stepping on each other.

The work is grounded in two long-running threads: The Coherence Company (transcript-native institutional intelligence, dreaming architectures, the Living Book) and Regentribe (decentralized knowledge infrastructure on Radicle and SurrealDB). Both feed the same question — how do we build a memory that grows.

§ 02
Research Six Active Threads

What we are studying now.

Six threads are alive in the repository at the moment. Each is a working investigation, not a finished position — open questions are tagged in the source and welcome challenge.

[ 01 ]
Memory Institutional intelligence

Transcript-native institutional intelligence.

Conversations as the source of truth — not documents as the artifact.

Building institutional memory from transcripts is not primarily a tool-calling problem; it is a memory-compilation problem. Conversations are high-volume, low-signal raw material that needs to be turned into a temporal graph of episodes, claims, contradictions, and unresolved questions — something a future agent (or human) can actually reason over, not just retrieve from.

→ research/The Coherence Company/agent-orchestration-and-memory-systems.md

[ 02 ]
Runtimes Harness design

Agent runtimes & the harness split.

The 2026 convergence: harness, execution, memory — three planes, not one chatbot.

Serious agent systems are separating the control plane (turns, approvals, tracing) from the execution plane (sandbox, files, ports) from the memory plane. We compare live runtimes — OpenClaw, HERMES, LangGraph, the OpenAI Agents SDK, and Microsoft Agent Framework — against transcript-first institutional workloads.

→ research/The Coherence Company/coco-openclaw-vs-hermes.md

[ 03 ]
Memory Consolidation

Dreaming & consolidation architectures.

Periodic sweeps that promote evidence into durable knowledge — wake, sleep, replay.

The frontier of agent memory is no longer "store more"; it is write-manage-read with explicit consolidation, update, and forgetting policies. We track the lineage from Dyna and DreamCoder through Generative Agents, Reflexion, ReasoningBank, ReMe, and OpenClaw's core Dreaming subsystem — looking for trustworthy, governed consolidation rather than poetic generation.

→ research/The Coherence Company/openclaw-dreams-and-hermes-dreaming-architecture.md

[ 04 ]
Substrate Knowledge graphs

Compiled memory & temporal graphs.

Pages, not chunks. Provenance, not retrieval.

The cleanest memory model is not "retrieve better chunks" but compile raw sources into a persistent, interlinked wiki that compounds over time. We benchmark SurrealDB, Neo4j Agent Memory, Zep / Graphiti, Letta, and Mem0 against the requirements for compiled, evidence-backed institutional knowledge with temporal facts and provenance.

→ research/regentribe/surrealdb-alternatives-research.md

[ 05 ]
Infrastructure Commons

Knowledge infrastructure for distributed communities.

GitHub for authoring, queryable graph for retrieval, MCP for any tool.

Genesis Brain Light is a two-layer system: members edit markdown in their own AI tools and push to GitHub; an automatic pipeline lifts those changes into a SurrealDB knowledge graph exposed via MCP. The aim is a knowledge commons that any AI assistant can query, regardless of which tool the human is working in.

→ research/regentribe/genesis-brain-light-design.md

[ 06 ]
Medium Publishing

The Living Book.

Knowledge that grows, not knowledge that ends.

A new medium for deep knowledge — where the author maintains authoritative coherence, the community adds annotation and extension, multiple coherence streams coexist as different lenses, and contributors are recognized for the value they add. The manifesto in the repo is one of the philosophical anchors for the rest of the work.

→ research/The Coherence Company/living-book-manifesto.md

§ 03
License CC0 1.0

A commons and a portfolio.

Everything in this repository is released under CC0 1.0 Universal — a public domain dedication. No copyright is asserted. No attribution is required. Contributions you make become part of the commons; do not contribute material you cannot release that way.

The choice is deliberate. A commons, because every artifact here is dedicated to the public domain so anyone can build on it. A portfolio, because everything you write here is signed by your commits and visible to anyone who wants to see how you think — your research, your syntheses, your judgment under uncertainty, the questions you choose to chase.

We work in the open so we can show our thinking, share research and synthesis, learn together, and show what each of us is capable of. Both readings are intended. Contribute work you would be proud to put your name on, and contribute it in a way that helps the next person.

Repository Four Artifacts

Four kinds of artifact.

The repo is organized so the kind of thinking is obvious from the path. Research is durable, citable knowledge. Plans are design specs and workstream breakdowns. Needs are open invitations. Scripts and skills are the shared tooling.

Skills are reusable agent capabilities — packaged instructions an AI collaborator pulls in to do a specific job consistently across the group. The folder is intentionally young: if you build something twice, package it as a skill.

§ 05
Method Operating Guide

How we collaborate.

When you sit down to contribute, the first thing you feel is the weight of this being public and permanent. Every word can be read, cited, and forked by anyone, forever. So you carry a quiet checklist with you: no PII, no names of private individuals — you use roles instead — no private chat excerpts, no credentials, no NDA-bound material. If it would be inappropriate on a public website, it does not belong here.

Next you notice that you are being treated as a research collaborator, not an assistant. The expectation is transparent, auditable work — explicit assumptions, structured markdown, inline citations, no hidden reasoning, no answer-first shortcuts. You write for the next person who will walk in cold.

When you start a real research task, the work moves through a familiar arc. You scan first — what is already there, what can you cite or supersede. Then you decompose the question into sub-questions, written into a notes file before you go exploring. Then you explore, gathering data and citing inline. Then synthesize — pulling threads into a document that stands on its own. Then reflect — leaving open-question markers for what is still unclear. And finally persist — a short dated entry in the topic's log so the trail is visible to whoever comes next. You append; you do not silently overwrite.

Throughout, citation and uncertainty are not optional. Load-bearing assumptions are tagged so the next reader can challenge them. You never fabricate a source — if you cannot verify it, you ask before citing it.

This is not a hackathon and it is not a Slack thread. It is closer to a public lab notebook that a small group keeps together — slow, append-only work over fast throwaway output; visible reasoning over polished conclusions; reproducibility for the next agent over efficiency for the current one.

§ 06
Onboard First Contribution

Getting started.

  • Read AGENT.md end to end. The rules of the road.
  • Skim research/ and plan/ for threads that interest you. Begin with the Living Book Manifesto for the philosophical frame; metis-hermes-01/00-workstreams-index.md for what is being designed.
  • Check needs/ for explicit roles to step into.
  • Pick a small first contribution — a research note, a citation cleanup, an open question you can answer. Follow the loop.
  • Open a PR with a short description: what you scanned, what you added, what remains open.
